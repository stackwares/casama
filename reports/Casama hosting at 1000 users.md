# Kimsufi undercuts Contabo at 64 GB

The cheapest host that can carry Casama at about 1,000 monthly users, on the planning case of 30 computers awake at 2 GB, is an OVH Kimsufi KS-5 with the 64 GB option at **€25.99 per month excluding VAT**. The load behind that choice is an estimate, because the docs do not record a real peak: 1,000 monthly users, computers at **1 GB** (the cost model’s current headless default) and **2 GB** (the cautious figure in the self-hosting guide), and peaks of **15, 30, and 100** awake. The primary cell is 30 awake at 2 GB, **about 66 GB of RAM including the platform**. Cheapest first, the five that hold about 64 GB are that KS-5, **Contabo Cloud VPS 16 at €29.60**, **Kimsufi KS-5-B with 64 GB at €29.99**, **the same KS-5-B with 128 GB at €33.99**, and **Kimsufi KS-6 at €38.99**, all excluding VAT, from pages and catalogs read on 11 November 2026. The **€4.40 Contabo Cloud VPS 4 stops being the winner** because that starter has 8 GB, and the smallest of these peaks already needs 21 GB. Model, image, and video API fees are per-use charges and stay out of this hosting bill.

## Thirty awake computers need about 66 GB

RAM follows computers that are awake, in use or sitting in a warm pool, and the monthly user count never becomes a concurrency figure in the docs. The cost model’s headless default is **1 vCPU and 1 GB**. The install guide still says to plan on **about 2 GB per running computer**, and the operations guide’s default `COMPUTER_MEMORY_MB` is **2048**, with the same “about 2 GB, plus the stack” sentence ([COSTS.md](file:///Users/nemoryoliver/Desktop/Projects/Kasama/docs/COSTS.md); [self-hosting guide](file:///Users/nemoryoliver/Desktop/Projects/Kasama/docs-site/self-hosting/index.md); [operations guide](file:///Users/nemoryoliver/Desktop/Projects/Kasama/docs-site/self-hosting/operations.md)). The two documents disagree, and neither page is dated against the other. The 2 GB column is the cautious one while the guide still prints 2048. The pages also disagree on idle sleep, 5 minutes in the cost model and 10 minutes in the operations guide. That changes how long an idle computer holds RAM. It leaves a stated awake count alone.

The six totals add **6 GB** once, for the API, the worker, and Postgres together. That 6 GB is an estimate set for this planning pass. It is not printed in the docs. It leaves out Caddy, Supabase Auth, REST, Realtime, the supervisor, egress, and TURN, which the install guide includes and for which no RAM figure exists. Warm-pool members count as awake RAM and sit inside the 15, 30, or 100 only when the operator put them there. A desktop at the cost model’s **4 GB** replaces the 1 GB or 2 GB term for that computer. The table assumes headless computers.

| Computers awake | At 1 GB | At about 2 GB |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 15 | **21 GB** | **36 GB** |
| 30 | **36 GB** | **66 GB** |
| 100 | **106 GB** | **206 GB** |

All six are estimates. **66 GB** is 30 × 2 GB plus the 6 GB allowance. A 64 GB machine sits 2 GB under that literal sum, and the sum already omits the rest of the stack, so 64 GB is the tight reading of the primary case and 96 GB or 128 GB is the reading with room. The **206 GB** cell is larger than every single machine in the five below. RAM is what orders them. The install floor is **40 GB or more**, of which images take about **6 GB**, and home folders and snapshots take the rest. The docs give no bytes per chat, per user, or per dump ([self-hosting guide](file:///Users/nemoryoliver/Desktop/Projects/Kasama/docs-site/self-hosting/index.md)). The smallest disk option in the five is 250 GB.

The ladder is short. **€3.61** separating KS-5 from Contabo is the price of leaving a 4-core Xeon and hard disks for 16 vCPU and flash, still at 64 GB, on a rate Contabo prints only for the first 24 months. **€0.39** past that Contabo card, the KS-5-B at 64 GB is a physical 6-core box with NVMe. **€4** past that, the same chassis at 128 GB is the machine that costs less than a Netcup 64 GB VPS, a Hetzner AX41, OVHcloud’s catalog VPS-5, and a pair of Hostinger KVM 8s. KS-6, another **€5**, buys 16 physical cores and 128 GB and returns to hard disks. Contabo Cloud VPS 18, **18 vCPU and 96 GB** at **€39.20 excluding VAT** (€49.00 at the page’s 25%), misses this five by **€0.21** and still misses the 106 GB estimate ([Contabo VPS](https://contabo.com/en/vps/)).

KS-5’s €25.99 is the Ireland catalog’s default monthly renew: the plan plus the 64 GB add-on. Applying the catalog’s 23% tax to it is **€31.97**, arithmetic on that rate, not a figure printed on a Kimsufi card. Stock of KS-5 and KS-5-B was not checked. A catalog price is not a delivery promise. European datacenter codes are on the plans, the port is **500 Mbit/s unmetered**, and each Kimsufi server includes a public IPv4. The Kimsufi FAQ gives the customer root on a machine with no virtualisation layer ([Kimsufi, Ireland](https://eco.ovhcloud.com/en-ie/kimsufi/); [Eco catalog, Ireland](https://eu.api.ovh.com/1.0/order/catalog/public/eco?ovhSubsidiary=IE)). Contabo’s Core VPS line is KVM with full root, which is the access the supervisor needs for the host Docker socket. Nested virtualization is blocked on that VPS line, and Docker on the host does not need it ([Contabo VPS](https://contabo.com/en/vps/); [Contabo nested virtualization](https://help.contabo.com/en/support/solutions/articles/103000271595-can-i-setup-nested-virtualization-on-my-server-)).

Four cores for 30 awake computers is a tighter share than Casama’s own dense-server sentence, which pairs **16 cores** with **about 28 standard computers** and says CPU can be shared because bots mostly wait on the model, so memory is the limit ([COSTS.md](file:///Users/nemoryoliver/Desktop/Projects/Kasama/docs/COSTS.md)). KS-5 wins the invoice. Contabo VPS 16 is the first machine in the five whose vCPU count sits near that 16-core sentence. KS-6 is the first physical 16-core machine, at €38.99, on hard disks.

## Eight gigabytes retires the €4 Contabo

The starter that wins at a few computers is **Contabo Cloud VPS 4 at €4.40 per month excluding VAT** for the first 24 months: **4 vCPU, 8 GB, 100 GB**. The install guide sizes that shape for a few computers at once and warns below about 8 GB ([Contabo VPS](https://contabo.com/en/vps/); [self-hosting guide](file:///Users/nemoryoliver/Desktop/Projects/Kasama/docs-site/self-hosting/index.md)). Every planning peak here starts above 8 GB. **21 GB** is the smallest cell in the table, 15 awake at 1 GB plus the 6 GB allowance. An 8 GB server cannot hold it, so the €4.40 price stops deciding the bill the moment the awake count leaves “a few.”

The next Contabo cards cover the smaller cells and still miss the primary one. **Cloud VPS 8 at €11.20 excluding VAT** is **8 vCPU and 24 GB**, enough for the 21 GB cell and short of every 36 GB cell. **Cloud VPS 12 at €20.00 excluding VAT** is **48 GB**, enough for both 36 GB cells (30 awake at 1 GB, or 15 awake at 2 GB) and short of 66 GB ([Contabo VPS](https://contabo.com/en/vps/)). The primary case is the first cell that asks for about 64 GB, which is where the five above take over from the starter list. The same page’s 24-month rate on VPS 16 is **€29.60**, about seven times the starter, for eight times the RAM, with the renewal after month 24 still unpublished.

## Conclusion

The hosting decision at this scale is smaller than the cost file suggests, and it is still conditional. Thirty awake headless computers fit a machine at **€25.99** or **€29.60** a month excluding VAT, so a budget copied from the file’s AX102 line pays **€259 plus €129 setup** for hardware this peak leaves idle. Inside the five, the live choice is whether 4 old cores and hard disks are an acceptable share of those 30 computers. The step to 16 vCPU and flash is **€3.61**, on a rate whose second term is unpublished.

What still dominates is the blank peak. A measured peak of 15 computers at 1 GB fits Contabo’s 24 GB VPS at **€11.20**. A measured peak of 100 computers at 2 GB fits none of the five, and the cheap extension is a second 128 GB Kimsufi at another **€33.99**. The five are the shortlist for the 30-at-2 GB case, and they come off the shortlist when a measured peak replaces that case.
