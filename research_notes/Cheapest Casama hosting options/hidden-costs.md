# Costs that change the cheapest always-on host

Pages below were fetched on 11 November 2026 unless a source gives its own date. Amounts are the published figure, not a currency conversion. Where a total is arithmetic on those figures, it is marked as calculated.

Casama's cost note, checked 2026-11-11, budgets a Hetzner CPX42 at €59.49–69.99 per month for 8 vCPU / 16 GB, with 20 TB then about €1/TB, and treats bot computers as Docker containers of about 1 vCPU / 1 GB on that same machine. That note is the claim being checked. It is not an independent price source. — [docs/COSTS.md](https://github.com/nemoryoliver/Kasama/blob/main/docs/COSTS.md) in this repo, "checked on 2026-11-11"

## Backup add-on percent

### Takeaway

Hetzner's published backup price is 20% of the server price per month for seven slots. Snapshots are a separate per-gigabyte charge. Primary IPv4 is €0.50 per month excluding VAT on top of a server that is not already quoted incl. IPv4. A customer based in Germany pays 19% VAT on the net price; an EU customer outside Germany with a valid VAT ID is reverse-charged and pays the net price. EU outbound overage is written on the price page as €1.00 inside a sentence that says "for each additional TB", while the price widget's own label says "per hour".

### Cited Findings

- Backups are "a monthly flat price, which is 20% of the price of the server you activate them for", and that price includes seven backup slots. The FAQ's worked example is an old €3.29 server, at €3.29 × 0.2 = €0.658 per month. The marketing page describes backups as automatic daily copies, seven slots, oldest replaced, server-bound, and removed when the server is deleted. It does not restate the percent. — [Hetzner Cloud billing FAQ](https://docs.hetzner.com/cloud/billing/faq/), last change 2020-07-07; [Hetzner Regular Performance](https://www.hetzner.com/cloud/regular-performance/), fetched 11 November 2026
- Snapshots are billed per gigabyte per month for the compressed size only, pro rata if the snapshot exists for part of a month. The FAQ's example is a 40 GB disk billed at "40GB or less". The FAQ does not state the euro-per-gigabyte rate. — [Hetzner Cloud billing FAQ](https://docs.hetzner.com/cloud/billing/faq/), fetched 11 November 2026
- Cloudtally, verified 10 November 2026, lists backups at 20% of the server price in all regions, snapshots at €0.0143 per GB per month, volumes at €0.0572 per GB per month, primary IPv4 at €0.0008 per hour and €0.50 per month, primary IPv6 at €0, floating IPv4 at €3.00 per month, and floating IPv6 at €1.00 per month. Its price-history table shows the snapshot rate moving from €0.0110 to €0.0143. — [cloudtally.eu](https://cloudtally.eu/hetzner-cloud-prices), verified 10 November 2026
- A 13 May 2026 archive of Hetzner's price-adjustment doc says all prices exclude VAT and lists the snapshot price changing from €0.0110 to €0.0143 per GB per month, and the volume price from €0.0440 to €0.0572, effective 1 April 2026. The live URL of that doc returned HTTP 404 on 11 November 2026. — [Archived Hetzner price adjustment](https://web.archive.org/web/20260513201413/https://docs.hetzner.com/general/infrastructure-and-availability/price-adjustment/), archived 13 May 2026; live URL checked 11 November 2026
- Outgoing traffic is the only traffic billed. Incoming traffic is free. Internal traffic is free, including other Hetzner Cloud servers in the same network zone via public IPs, private networks, and Hetzner dedicated servers. Overage is billed in 100 MB blocks, rounded up. Alerts at 75% and 100% of included traffic do not stop usage. — [Hetzner Cloud billing FAQ](https://docs.hetzner.com/cloud/billing/faq/); [Hetzner traffic doc](https://docs.hetzner.com/robot/general/traffic/), traffic doc last change 2024-05-21, both fetched 11 November 2026
- The traffic doc says root servers are charged "€ 1 ($1.20)/TB" for overusage, lists CX, CPX, and CAX at 20 TB for EU, and says cloud overage is charged in blocks of 100 MB (0.0001 TB). The cloud section does not repeat a separate euro-per-TB cloud rate. — [Hetzner traffic doc](https://docs.hetzner.com/robot/general/traffic/), last change 2024-05-21, fetched 11 November 2026
- On the live Regular Performance and Cost-Optimized pages, the sentence reads "For each additional TB at EU-Central locations we charge" and the price widget inside that sentence is €1.00 excl. VAT. The widget's accessible text is "€ 1.00 per hour, excl. VAT", its attribute is `price-type="hourly"`, and its product key is `CLOUD_66` with `location=ALL`. The Singapore widget in the same sentence pattern is "€ 7.40 per hour, excl. VAT" with `location=SIN1`. — [Hetzner Regular Performance](https://www.hetzner.com/cloud/regular-performance/), fetched 11 November 2026
- Cloudtally lists server traffic overage at €1.00 per TB for Germany and the US and €7.40 per TB for Singapore, verified 10 November 2026. — [cloudtally.eu](https://cloudtally.eu/hetzner-cloud-prices)
- VAT: "Our net prices are the same for all customers." Clients based in Germany are charged 19% VAT. Customers in other EU member states with a valid VAT ID are invoiced under the reverse-charge procedure, without German tax. EU customers without a valid VAT ID pay their own country's rate. The table lists Finland at 25.50% starting 1 September 2024. Customers in countries outside the EU that are not on a named list are invoiced without VAT. The Germany rule is a separate heading from the reverse-charge heading. — [Hetzner value added tax](https://docs.hetzner.com/general/billing-and-account-management/billing-at-hetzner/value-added-tax/), fetched 11 November 2026
- Cloud host systems have a redundant 10 Gbit connection shared by the instances on the host. Hetzner does not offer a bandwidth guarantee and says to expect about 300–500 Mbit. — [Hetzner Regular Performance](https://www.hetzner.com/cloud/regular-performance/), fetched 11 November 2026

### Inferences

- Calculated from the FAQ's 20% rule and Cloudtally's 10 November net server prices: backup for a full month is €13.90 on a Germany CPX42 (€69.49 × 0.2) and €11.80 on a Finland CPX42 (€58.99 × 0.2). The same rule on CPX32 is €7.10 in Germany (€35.49 × 0.2) and €6.00 in Finland (€29.99 × 0.2). Because IPv4 is a separate invoice line, the 20% base is the server price, not the €0.50 IPv4 line. Twenty percent of the incl.-IPv4 display would be €0.10 higher.
- Calculated German-resident totals on the incl.-IPv4 display, before backups: CPX42 Nuremberg €69.99 × 1.19 = €83.29; CPX42 Helsinki €59.49 × 1.19 = €70.79; CPX32 Nuremberg €35.99 × 1.19 = €42.83; CPX32 Helsinki €30.49 × 1.19 = €36.28. A Finnish resident without a VAT ID on the Helsinki CPX42 pays €59.49 × 1.255 = €74.66. An EU company outside Germany with a valid VAT ID pays the net excl.-VAT figure. A customer based in Germany, including a German company, falls under the 19% heading rather than the reverse-charge heading.
- Calculated snapshot ceiling at Cloudtally's €0.0143 per GB: a 320 GB CPX42 disk that does not compress is €4.58 per month. The FAQ says the bill is the compressed size, so a real snapshot is that amount or less. One snapshot of the disk is cheaper than the 20% backup on a CPX42. The backup buys seven daily slots and is deleted with the server. A snapshot remains until deleted, including after the server is gone.
- The €1.00 figure in the cost note matches the price page's "for each additional TB … €1.00" and the traffic doc's €1/TB sentence. The widget label "per hour" contradicts the sentence it sits in. €1 per hour would be a different product from "about €1/TB". The €7.40 Singapore figure matches Cloudtally's per-TB Singapore rate, which supports reading both widgets as per TB.

### Gaps

- No live Hetzner doc page states €0.0143. The current price-adjustment URL 404s. The rate rests on the 13 May 2026 archive and on Cloudtally's 10 November 2026 table.
- The FAQ's 20% example still uses a €3.29 server. Nothing fetched on 11 November 2026 replaces the 20% rule, and nothing fetched says the percent changed in the June 2026 server price adjustment.
- The official pages do not say whether backup's 20% is calculated on the incl.-IPv4 display or on the server line only. The FAQ says "the price of the server", and IPv4 is a separate resource.
- Whether the EU overage unit is per TB or per hour is contradicted on the same official page, as cited above. The traffic doc's cloud section does not print its own per-TB number.

## What fails if we put only the API on a tiny box and Postgres on Supabase's $25 plan: total vs one VPS

### Takeaway

Supabase Pro is $25 per month for one Micro project, and Micro is a shared CPU with 1 GB of RAM and a 60-connection limit. Adding that bill to a tiny Hetzner API box costs more than one orderable CPX32 that keeps Postgres on the machine, and the tiny box still cannot host the bot computers. The $25 plan stops being $25 if the database needs the Small or Medium compute size, an IPv4 add-on, or a second project.

### Cited Findings

- Supabase Pro is "From $25 / month", with the first project included and additional projects from $10 per month. Paid plans include "$10/mo in compute credits, enough to cover one Micro instance." The pricing estimator shows plan $25, compute $10, credits −$10, total $25 for one Micro project. Quotas on Pro: 100,000 monthly active users, then $0.00325 per MAU; 8 GB disk per project, then $0.125 per GB; 250 GB egress, then $0.09 per GB; 250 GB cached egress, then $0.03 per GB; 100 GB file storage, then $0.0213 per GB; daily backups stored for 7 days. The Pro spend cap is on by default for usage beyond the included quota. — [Supabase pricing](https://supabase.com/pricing), fetched 11 November 2026
- Compute sizes on the docs page: Micro $0.01344 per hour, about $10 per month, shared CPU, 1 GB memory, recommended max database size 10 GB; Small $0.0206 per hour, about $15, shared CPU, 2 GB, 50 GB recommended; Medium $0.0822 per hour, about $60, shared CPU, 4 GB, 100 GB recommended. Sizes through Medium are shared CPU. Large and above are dedicated vCPU. Compute changes usually cause less than 2 minutes of downtime and are not automatic. — [Supabase compute and disk](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/compute-and-disk), fetched 11 November 2026
- Connection caps on that page: Micro allows 60 database connections and 200 pooler clients. Small allows 90 and 400. Medium allows 120 and 600. — [Supabase compute and disk](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/compute-and-disk), fetched 11 November 2026
- Free projects are paused after 1 week of inactivity, with a limit of 2 active projects. The Free compute line is shared CPU and up to 500 MB RAM, with a 500 MB database. — [Supabase pricing](https://supabase.com/pricing), fetched 11 November 2026
- The IPv4 add-on is "$0.0055 per hour ($4 per month)". Direct connections and the dedicated pooler are IPv6 unless the add-on is enabled. The shared pooler is IPv4 without the add-on. Each read replica adds another IPv4 charge. The IPv4 address doc says it was updated 29 October 2026. — [Supabase IPv4 usage](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/manage-your-usage/ipv4); [Supabase IPv4 address](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/ipv4-address), fetched 11 November 2026
- Orderable Hetzner boxes small enough to be "API only", prices excl. VAT and incl. IPv4: CPX02, 1 vCPU, 1 GB, 20 GB, HEL1 €5.49 and FSN1+NBG1 €6.49; CPX12, 1 vCPU, 2 GB, 40 GB, HEL1 €9.99 and FSN1+NBG1 €11.99; CPX22, 2 vCPU, 4 GB, 80 GB, HEL1 €16.99 and NBG1 €19.99. CPX32, the one-VPS size for API plus Postgres plus one or two 1 GB bots, is HEL1 €30.49 and NBG1 €35.99. — [Hetzner Regular Performance](https://www.hetzner.com/cloud/regular-performance/), fetched 11 November 2026
- Hetzner EU servers include at least 20 TB of outgoing traffic. The additional-TB widget shows €1.00 excl. VAT, with the unit conflict noted in the backup section. Supabase egress past 250 GB is $0.09 per GB. — [Hetzner Regular Performance](https://www.hetzner.com/cloud/regular-performance/); [Supabase pricing](https://supabase.com/pricing), fetched 11 November 2026

### Inferences

- Calculated Supabase totals before tax, one project, inside the disk, egress, and MAU quotas: Micro stays $25 because the $10 credit covers the $10 Micro instance. Small is $25 + $15 − $10 = $30. Medium is $25 + $60 − $10 = $75. Enabling the IPv4 add-on adds $4. A second Micro project adds about $10 after the single $10 credit is used, which matches the pricing page's "additional projects from $10/mo" and its own example shape of $25 plus compute minus one credit.
- A split of CPX22 in Helsinki at €16.99 plus Supabase Micro at $25 is two invoices. The euro piece is €13.50 less than a Helsinki CPX32 at €30.49, and the added invoice is $25. At any euro-dollar rate near parity, the split costs more than the single CPX32 and still has only 4 GB on the Docker host. No 11 November 2026 exchange rate was collected, so this is a comparison of the two currencies side by side, not a converted total.
- Putting the API on CPX02 (1 GB) or CPX12 (2 GB) does not leave a gigabyte per bot computer. The worker that starts bot containers has to run on a Docker host. Supabase replaces Postgres. It does not replace that host. The cost note's computers stay on the VPS.
- What gets worse if Postgres moves: each query is a round trip off the machine and counts toward Supabase's 250 GB egress, then $0.09 per GB. On one Hetzner server that traffic is local, and public egress is inside 20 TB before the about-€1/TB charge. Micro's 60 direct connections are a hard cap for an API, a worker, and bots opening their own clients. The shared pooler raises the client cap to 200 and is the IPv4 path that avoids the $4 add-on.
- What the $25 plan does buy that a bare VPS does not: the project is not paused for inactivity, and daily backups are kept for 7 days. A single Hetzner VPS has no backup until the 20% add-on or a separate snapshot is turned on. Free Supabase is not an always-on database, because inactivity pauses it after a week.
- Medium compute at about $75 before tax is already above the Helsinki CPX42 display price of €59.49 excluding VAT. A database that outgrows Micro removes the reason to split.
