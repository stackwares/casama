## Why people switch from OpenClaw

OpenClaw is free, MIT-licensed and reaches about 29 chat apps. It runs on your own machine, with its
sandbox and approval prompts off until you turn them on. That suits a careful person on a spare
computer. It's the part most alternatives change:

- **Where the agent works.** Agent Zero, NanoClaw, Rakazo, OpenBot and Casama keep the agent in a
  container or a computer of its own, so a mistake stays there.
- **When it asks.** Casama and Rakazo ask before risky steps by default, OpenBot uses admin
  policies, NanoClaw asks before installs and new tools, and Hermes Agent asks about risky-looking
  shell commands. Agent Zero and nanobot don't ask.
- **How big it is.** NanoClaw and nanobot were written as much smaller takes on OpenClaw.

## Questions

### What is the best free alternative to OpenClaw?

Hermes Agent, nanobot, NanoClaw and Agent Zero are free and open source like OpenClaw. Casama is
free to self-host and has a free hosted plan. You pay for the AI model unless you run a local one.

### Which OpenClaw alternative is the most secure?

The one whose defaults match how much setup you'll do. If you won't configure a sandbox and
approvals yourself, pick one that keeps the agent off your machine and asks first by default.

### Is there a hosted OpenClaw alternative?

Yes. Casama, Rakazo and MausBot have hosted plans, and Casama's has a free tier. Hermes Agent has an
optional hosted agent through Nous Portal.

### Which alternative is closest to OpenClaw?

Hermes Agent: also MIT-licensed, on your own machine, in more than 20 chat apps. See
[OpenClaw vs Hermes Agent](/compare/openclaw-vs-hermes-agent).
