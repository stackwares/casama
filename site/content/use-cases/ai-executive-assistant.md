## What the bot does

- **Preps meetings.** A one-page brief before each one: who, what was agreed last time, what is open.
- **Drafts for you.** Replies wait in your drafts; only you press Send.
- **Guards the calendar.** Spots clashes and offers new times with a button to do it.
- **Chases.** Keeps a list of what you're waiting on and nudges when it's late.
- **Holds the noise.** Non-urgent notifications wait out quiet hours.

## Where it stops to ask

- Sending, on your behalf, to anyone.
- Moving a meeting that involves other people, if you set it to ask.
- Any payment.

You set these rules per bot and per action. See [how approvals work](/security).

## What it looks like

1. 08:00: a brief with today's five meetings and one decision you owe.
2. 09:45: "Maya asked for a slot this week. I drafted a reply with two open times."
3. 13:50: ten minutes before the board call, the brief arrives with last quarter's actions.
4. 18:00: "Three threads are waiting on you. Want replies drafted?"

## Why its own computer matters

Each Casama bot works on a sandboxed Linux machine of its own, with a browser, files and a shell.
It keeps going when you close your laptop, it can't reach your own files, and you can watch its
screen or take over at any time. Before a command that deletes data, its computer is saved, so the
work can be undone.

## Set it up

1. Create a bot with the **Assistant** persona, and add the **Briefing** persona's morning routine.
2. Connect mail, calendar and your documents.
3. Turn on check-ins so it can suggest work, with **Do It** and **Not Now** buttons.
4. Set quiet hours and which kinds of message may interrupt you.

Ready-made skills, starter bots and routines are in the [gallery](https://gallery.casama.ai).

## Questions

### How is this different from a calendar assistant?

It works across mail, calendar and documents, remembers context between days, and runs on its own schedule.

### Can my human assistant use it too?

Yes. Put the bot in a group chat or a shared workspace, with roles for who may change what.

### Does it join meetings?

Meeting notes can come from the note-taking tools you already use; see the docs on meeting notes.
