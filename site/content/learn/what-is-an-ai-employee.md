## What makes it an "employee" and not a chatbot

| | Chatbot | AI employee |
|---|---|---|
| Job | Whatever you type | One standing role |
| Starts work | When you ask | On a schedule, or when something arrives |
| Tools | Few or none | The apps and files the role needs |
| Memory | This conversation | Your preferences, your customers, past work |
| Place to work | The chat box | A computer: yours, or one of its own |
| Supervision | You read every reply | You approve the risky steps and review the rest |

## Jobs that suit one

Work is a good fit when it repeats, has a clear "done", and can be checked afterwards:

- **Support**: answer common questions from your help documents; pass on the rest.
- **Inbox and calendar**: sort, draft, propose times, chase replies.
- **Research**: a weekly price or competitor check with sources.
- **Admin**: forms, data entry, matching receipts, chasing invoices.
- **Reporting**: a Monday summary pulled from several tools.
- **Coding chores**: run a coding agent on a failing test and report back.

## Jobs that don't

- Anything where a wrong answer is costly and hard to notice: legal advice, medical decisions,
  final sign-off on money.
- Work that depends on relationships or taste.
- One-off tasks that take longer to explain than to do.

## What "hiring" one involves

1. **Write the job down.** One role per AI employee. "Answer support email from the help docs, and
   never promise a refund" beats "help with customers".
2. **Give it the tools for that job only.** The support bot doesn't need your bank.
3. **Decide what it must ask.** Sending, paying and deleting should wait for a person at first.
4. **Set a budget.** Per task and per day, so a confused run can't spend much.
5. **Review for a week.** Read what it did, correct it, and let it remember the corrections.
6. **Loosen slowly.** Allow the actions it has got right many times.

## What it costs

There is no salary, but each step uses an AI model, and a computer costs something to run.
A typical short task costs cents. A long research task can cost a dollar or more. Compare prices by
what is included: model usage, computer hours, seats, and what happens when you go over.

## Where they go wrong

- **Confidently wrong.** A model can state something false. Sources and a record of each step let
  you check.
- **Tricked by content.** An email or web page can contain instructions. Good software treats that
  content as untrusted and asks before sending data out.
- **Stuck in a loop.** Limits on repeated failures and on spending stop a run that isn't getting
  anywhere.
- **Over-trusted.** The failures that hurt come from giving broad access on day one.
