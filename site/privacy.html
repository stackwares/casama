2. What we collect

Account information

  • Your email address, and your name if you give it or your sign-in provider shares it.
  • If you sign in with Apple or Google, an identifier from that provider. With Apple you can choose to hide your email.
  • Your password, stored only as a secure hash, and your two-step sign-in authenticators if you set them up.
  • Your profile: the name bots should call you, pronouns, how you like replies, what you write under About you, and facts bots learn about you if Learn about me from chats is on.
  • Your profile photo, if you add one. If you sign in with Google and have no photo yet, we copy your Google profile photo once and store it with your account. You can replace or remove it at any time, and a removed photo isn't copied again.

Your content

Everything you and your bots create in Casama: conversations, messages, attachments, memories, skills, routines, pages, tickets, knowledge sources, files on your bots' computers, voice transcripts, and the history of each run (tool calls, outputs and errors).

Keys, secrets and connections

Model keys, app connection tokens and secrets you give your bots. These are kept in an encrypted vault on the server. They are never shown back to you in full, never put into a model's input, and never included in exports.

Device data, only if you turn it on

Calendar, contacts, health summaries, named places, notifications from apps you pick, and computer activity summaries. Each kind is off until you switch it on. See Device data and health.

Voice

Microphone audio when you dictate, read aloud, or start a live call. The wake word on desktop and Android is detected on your device, and no audio leaves it until a call starts.

Purchases

Your plan, subscription status, credit balance and purchase records, such as store transaction and order ids. Card details are handled by Apple, Google or Dodo Payments. We never see your full card number.

Invites

Your invite code, and who signed up with whose code, so we can give out invite credits. Anyone with your code or invite link sees your first name, if you set one. You see how many friends joined, not who they are.

Usage and technical data

  • Usage records: model tokens, computer time, costs and similar counts, so we can show usage and bill credits.
  • An audit log of admin actions, approvals and credential changes in your workspace.
  • Push notification tokens for your devices, and basic device and app details such as platform and app version.
  • Request data our servers need to work and stay safe, such as IP addresses.
  • Signals that protect sign-up and our computers from abuse, such as a sign-up check token and blocked network attempts from a bot's computer.

Error reports and usage analytics

Official Casama apps and Casama Cloud send crash and error reports to diagnose issues. An app sends them to one service at a time, which may be Sentry, PostHog or, for the official iPhone, iPad, Android and Mac apps, Google's Firebase Crashlytics; we can change which one without an app update. A report holds what crashed and where in the app's code, the app version, device model, operating system and free memory, with no account id, email or message content. On Casama Cloud, official apps and servers also collect usage and product interaction events (such as screens viewed, features used, milestones reached, and daily activity rollups) to PostHog, and the same events, without the workspace rollups, to Google Analytics 4 (through Firebase in the apps), to understand how the product is used and improve reliability. Google Analytics runs without advertising ids, Google Signals or ad personalization. You can turn off usage analytics and crash reports at any time with Share usage data and crash reports in Settings → Privacy; the apps then send neither (unsent crash reports on the device are deleted). Reports and events are scrubbed: we never collect message text, prompts, personal content, secrets, device files, or audio in analytics. Workspace admins can turn usage data off for a whole workspace, and deleting your account deletes your analytics. When an official app is connected to a server run by someone else, it sends only crash reports and one anonymous “the app was used today” ping a day. Apps you build yourself send nothing unless you explicitly configure them.

When you contact us

Your email address and whatever you write to us.

Casama's monthly email

An optional email about what's new and what's next in Casama, once a month at most. You get it only if you opt in: with the switch in Settings (Casama's monthly email), the box when you create an account, or the form on casama.ai (which asks you to confirm your address first). We then keep your email address and where you signed up on our mailing list, which Resend holds and sends from. Every email has a link to unsubscribe, and you can turn it off in the app at any time. Deleting your account removes you from the list.

This website

casama.ai sets no cookies and runs no ad or tracking scripts. It counts page views without identifying anyone: which page was opened and which kind of site led there (a search engine, an AI assistant, another site). No visitor ID or IP address is kept with the count.

When a page on casama.ai, docs.casama.ai, gallery.casama.ai or the web app at app.casama.ai breaks with a script error, the page loads Sentry's error reporter and sends the error, the page address (without anything after ? or #), and your browser and operating system. Nothing loads until something breaks, nothing is stored on your device, and nothing is sent if your browser sends Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control, or (in the web app) if you turned off Share usage data and crash reports or the web app sends its crash reports to another service.

Our server also passes that same anonymous count to Google Analytics, for casama.ai, docs.casama.ai, gallery.casama.ai, casama.link and status.casama.ai. No Google script runs on these pages and nothing is stored on your device. Google receives the page, the referring site, the country (from Cloudflare), whether the device is a phone, and a few clicks that show whether the site works: opening the web app, a download or store button, the QR code, an invite or gallery link (never the invite code), playing the film, and copying the install command. These come with an ID our server works out from the day, your IP address and your browser, so Google can tell that pages seen the same day came from the same browser. The ID changes every day, can't be turned back into your IP address, and no IP address is sent. Links from the site into the app or onto Google Play say which page they came from (for example utm_source=casama.ai). We use this to see which pages help people and how search brings them here. Google's handling is described in Google's Privacy Policy. The apps use Google Analytics only as described above for Casama Cloud.

Cloudflare, which serves the site, processes standard request data such as IP addresses to deliver it and block attacks.

4. AI models and what they see

To answer you, a bot sends the conversation and relevant context (such as memories, skills, tool results, your profile, and device data you allowed) to an AI model provider. Health summaries are part of that context unless you have marked Health as Private models only.

  • Casama Cloud. On Casama Cloud we choose the models, and requests go through our accounts. We send only what the request needs. These are the AI providers your content may be sent to, and what each one gets:
    • OpenRouter (the routing service we send model requests through). It receives the conversation and context for bots' replies, your prompts for images, audio for dictation and text for read aloud, memory search and video, and passes them to the model maker that runs the model. We set every request to use only providers that don't collect or train on prompts, and our account is set to block providers that train on prompts or publish them.
    • DeepSeek (through OpenRouter). Its model writes bots' replies and does background work such as summaries and Casama Help answers. It receives the conversation and context described above, to write the reply.
    • Google (through OpenRouter). Its models make images, read replies aloud and make video. They receive your prompt, or the text to read aloud, to produce the result.
    • OpenAI (through OpenRouter). Its models turn your dictation into text and turn memories and search words into numbers for memory search. They receive your audio, or the text of a memory, for that purpose only.
    • Google Gemini Live (directly, not through OpenRouter). Live voice calls: your app connects to Google with a short-lived key, and your voice and the call's context go to Google to produce the spoken reply.
    • Web search: DeepSeek, Parallel and Serper. When a bot searches the web, only the search words are sent, to one of these providers (the next is tried if one doesn't answer). Pages the bot then opens are fetched by our own servers.
    Each provider processes what it receives to produce its result for us, under its own terms. This list changes when we change models; the models Casama uses today are all from the makers named here.
  • Your own key. If you add your own key for a provider (on Casama Cloud, for maps or data services), requests to it go to that provider under your account and their terms and privacy policy.
  • Local models. If you point a bot at a model on your own machine or network, the content stays there.

What models never see:

  • Secrets. Bots use placeholders. The real value is filled in only when a request goes to a host the secret allows, after the model has written the call. Known secret values are removed before anything reaches a model, the run history or logs.
  • Model keys and connection tokens. These stay in the server's vault and are used by the server, not the bot.
  • Card details for bot payments. Bots see placeholders only.
  • What you type while you control a bot's computer. While you hold control, input is not recorded and screenshots are not sent to the model.

Model providers have their own policies about storing and using the data they receive. Check them for the providers you choose.

Casama Help on our websites. The Casama Help chat on casama.ai and docs.casama.ai sends your messages to our AI model provider to answer them. We keep no transcript, only daily counters and, without any identity attached, questions it couldn't answer so we can improve the FAQ.

5. Device data and health

Device connections let bots use personal data from your phone or computer. These rules apply:

  • Each kind is off until you turn it on. The system permission prompt appears only then.
  • Turning a kind on doesn't give any bot access. A bot must ask, and you tap Allow. You can revoke a bot at any time.
  • A bot reads your device data only in runs acting for you. A teammate using the same bot gets nothing, and workspace admins can't see it.
  • Every read shows on the run's timeline: which kind, how many items, which dates and which devices.
  • You can mark a kind Private models only, so only bots whose models all run on your own machine or private network can read it.
  • Bots don't learn facts about you from runs that used personal device data.

What each kind shares:

KindWhat's shared
CalendarOne summary per day, from yesterday to 30 days ahead. Bots can add events only after asking, unless your action rules allow it.
ContactsNames, phone numbers and email addresses.
HealthDaily summaries (sleep, steps, active energy, workouts, resting heart rate, heart-rate variability, weight) and workout summaries. Cycle tracking, blood glucose and blood pressure are separate switches, off by default. Raw samples never leave your device.
PlacesArriving at and leaving places you name. We keep the place name and times, not a location trail. For "leave now" reminders, your current location is asked for once, used to work out travel time, then deleted.
Notifications (Android)Notifications from apps you pick only. One-time codes are masked on your device and again on the server.
Computer activity (desktop)Off by default. Minutes per app and the top window titles, per hour. Never your keystrokes, selections or screenshots.
Quick ask context (desktop)Read only when you press the quick ask shortcut: the front app, window title, selected text and browser tab. You can switch each part off before sending. A screenshot is taken only if you ask.

By default, daily summaries are kept for a year, and places and notifications for 90 days. You can shorten this for each kind, or choose "live only" so nothing is stored. Turning a kind off deletes what it stored. Forgetting a device deletes everything it shared.

Health data. On Casama Cloud, health data is stored only in encrypted form, and only once you have turned on encryption for a workspace you own. A bot can send the summaries you allowed to the AI model it runs on. Mark Health Private models only and only a model on your own machine or private network can read them. The model provider's own policy then applies to what it receives. We never use health data for advertising or marketing, never sell it, and never use it to train AI models. Bots don't give medical advice.

7. Legal bases

Where the law requires a legal basis for processing, we rely on:

  • Contract: to provide the service you signed up for.
  • Consent: for device data, health data, Casama's monthly email, and anything else you switch on. You can withdraw consent at any time by switching it off.
  • Legitimate interests: to keep Casama secure, prevent abuse and fix problems, in ways you would reasonably expect.
  • Legal obligation: for example, keeping billing records.

8. How long we keep it

  • Your account and content: until you delete them, or delete your account.
  • Run history: 180 days by default. Workspace owners and admins can change this from 30 to 730 days in the app, or 7 to 3,650 days through the API. Older run events are deleted automatically every day.
  • Device data: as described in Device data and health.
  • Billing records: as long as tax and accounting laws require.
  • Casama's monthly email: until you unsubscribe or delete your account. When you unsubscribe, your address stays on the list marked as unsubscribed, so you don't get it again; deleting your account removes it.
  • Backups: Casama Cloud keeps encrypted backup copies for 14 days, then deletes them.

When you delete your account

  • Right away: you are signed out everywhere, your devices stop getting notifications, and your device data is deleted.
  • After a 7-day grace period: your account, and every workspace where you are the only owner, with its bots, chats and computers (including computer snapshots). The app shows the exact date.
  • Shared workspaces with another owner keep their data. Only your membership is removed.

A workspace where you are the only owner is deleted with your account, even if other people use it. Make someone else an owner first if it should stay.

After your account is purged, backup copies of that data are deleted on the same 14-day cycle.

9. Your rights and choices

Wherever you live, you can:

  • Access and export your data. In the app, go to Settings → Privacy → Export my data, or run casama account export. You get a ZIP of JSON files with everything you can see. Secrets and keys appear by name only.
  • Correct your data. Edit your profile and content in the app, or ask us.
  • Delete your data. Delete individual items in the app, or your whole account at Settings → Privacy → Delete my account. Without the app, see casama.ai/delete-account.
  • Object to or restrict how we use your data, and withdraw consent for anything you switched on.
  • Be informed about how we process your data, which is what this policy is for.
  • Complain to a data protection authority. In the Philippines, that is the National Privacy Commission (privacy.gov.ph). You can also complain to the authority where you live.

For anything you can't do in the app, email privacy@casama.ai. We may need to confirm it's you. We aim to reply within 30 days. We won't treat you differently for using these rights.

If your data is in a workspace run by your employer or another organization, they control that workspace. We may refer your request to them.

10. Security

We protect your data with:

  • Encrypted connections (TLS) everywhere.
  • Row-level security on every database table, so one workspace can't read another's data.
  • Keys and secrets in an encrypted vault, never returned by the API.
  • Optional encryption at rest for workspace content, with a key per workspace.
  • Two-step sign-in, which workspaces can require for owners and admins.
  • Isolated computers for bots, with a network proxy that blocks private and internal addresses.
  • Approvals before risky bot actions, and checkpoints before destructive commands.

No system is perfectly secure. If a breach affects your personal data, we will notify you and the National Privacy Commission as the law requires. To report a security issue, email security@casama.ai.

11. International transfers

When data moves across borders, we use providers that commit to protecting it, and we take the steps the law requires, such as contractual safeguards.

13. Self-hosted servers and telemetry

If you use a Casama server that you or someone else runs, the data on it stays on that server. The person or organization running it is responsible for it. Ask them about their privacy practices.

A self-hosted server sends us nothing by default. If its owner turns on the usage ping (TELEMETRY=on), it sends us once a week: a random instance id, the Casama version, and the number of bots and users active in the last 7 days. Nothing else.

If a self-hosted server uses Casama Link for our cloud computers or model credits, the data needed for those requests passes through Casama Cloud and this policy applies to it.

If its owner turns on remote access with Casama Link, the server is linked to a Casama Cloud workspace and gets an address on casama.link. We keep a record of each linked server: its name, a random instance id, its address, the platform and Casama version it runs, and when it last checked in. Traffic between your phone and the server runs through a Cloudflare tunnel straight to that server, not through our servers. Notifications the server sends to the official apps pass through Casama Cloud on their way to Apple or Google and are not stored; with private notifications turned on they carry no message text. Live view away from the server uses short-lived credentials for Cloudflare's relay. Sign-in by QR code happens between the app and the server, so we never see the code.

The official apps may still use Apple or Google services (such as push notifications) when connected to a self-hosted server, as described above.

15. Casama for Chrome, Edge and Firefox

Casama for Chrome is a browser extension that connects your browser to your Casama bots. The same extension runs in Microsoft Edge, and a version for Firefox does everything below except bots in your browser, the browser as a device, recording a tab and meeting notes; this section covers all of them. It reads a page only when you're asking about it or saving it: when you start a message in its panel or switch on one of its chips, use its right-click menu, keyboard shortcut or address bar keyword, or save something. Switching tabs only tells it the new tab's address and title, to show on its chips. Nothing leaves your browser until you press Send or save, it never reads pages in the background, and it never records your browsing unless you turn on one of the "browser as a device" features below.

  • What it sends, and when. With a message, the page's address and title and the text you selected, unless you switch them off first. The page's text, a PDF's text, a video's captions or a screenshot only when you switch them on for that message. Files and screenshots you add. "What was sent" under each message shows exactly what went.
  • Where it goes. Only to the Casama server you signed in to (Casama Cloud, or a self-hosted server), where it becomes part of the conversation and is handled as described in this policy. The bot's AI model sees it as part of your message.
  • What it never reads. Casama's own pages (including the addresses of the servers you sign in to), and pages with a password or card field, which it also never screenshots or saves. Banking and payment, health, password manager and government ID sites are also off by default, and you can add your own sites to the never-list.
  • What it keeps in your browser. Your sign-in (the long-lived token on disk, short-lived tokens in memory only), your settings, a short record of what went with recent messages, and saves waiting to be sent while you're offline. Nothing is synced to your other computers. Signing out deletes the sign-in and this browser's notification registration.
  • On pages. If you turn on the selection bar, the write button, site helpers or highlights, a small script runs on the sites you gave the extension access to, to show those buttons. It reads nothing and sends nothing until you tap one; then it works like asking in the panel. It never runs on the pages listed above as never read.
  • Highlights, page commands and chats about a page. Text you highlight (with a little of the text around it, to find it again), the page's address and title, and your page commands are kept in your account on your Casama server; bots acting for you can read your highlights. A conversation you start from a page remembers that page's address. The extension also keeps a small list of those pages in your browser, to show "This page before" without asking the server.
  • Other tabs. If you add another tab with "@" or send a set of tabs, the extension asks Chrome once to see your tabs' titles and addresses, and reads a tab only when you pick it.
  • On your computer. Quick answers made with Chrome's built-in AI model, translating a page in place, reading it aloud, and trimming a long page before it's sent all happen in your browser and send nothing.
  • Live calls. A call from the panel streams your voice to the bot's speech model (Google's Gemini Live) with a single-use key from your server, as calls in the Casama apps do; what was said is saved to the conversation.
  • Bots in your browser. If you pair the browser and pick bots, those bots can open pages and act in it for chats and routines that are yours, through Chrome's debugging interface. Each works only in its own tab group; the extension never reads or acts on your other tabs. What a bot reads in its tabs (page text, an accessibility outline of the page, screenshots, files it downloads) goes to your Casama server as part of that conversation, like anything else the bot does. Text a page hides from people is left out. Each step that changes something (opening a page, clicking, typing, uploading, downloading) is kept in your workspace's audit log with the site's address. Sites you always or never allow are kept with the paired browser in your account. Bots never type passwords, card numbers or one-time codes; purchases and password changes are left to you. Recording a skill by showing keeps the pages you opened, what you clicked and what you typed, except passwords. Unpairing deletes the paired browser and its site lists.
  • This browser as a device. Each of these is off until you turn it on in the extension's settings, and only bots you allow can ask. The tab in front: when such a bot asks (or the Casama desktop app's quick ask on Windows and Linux), its address, title, your selection and, with site access, its text go to your server for that answer only and are not stored. History and bookmarks: when such a bot searches, at most 50 matching addresses and titles go, for that answer only, not stored. Starting routines: when you open a site one of your routines names, a download finishes or you add a bookmark, that one event (the site's name, the file's name or the bookmark's title) goes to your server, only when one of your routines starts on it, never for banking, health, password manager, government ID or Casama's own sites. Activity recap: minutes per site, per hour, with site names only (never pages, addresses or titles), kept 30 days for your recaps. Private models only: with this on, what the browser shares and page content you send from the panel go only to bots whose AI models all run privately; otherwise Chrome's built-in model answers in your browser, or nothing is sent.
  • Bookmarks. "Bring in your bookmarks" sends your bookmarks' titles and addresses (and your reading list, if you choose) to a bot's knowledge on your server, once, when you press it. Nothing syncs after that.
  • Recording a tab. When you press Record, the extension records the tab you're on, with its sound, for up to two minutes, and the video goes with your next message like any file. Chrome shows that the tab is being shared while it records.
  • Meeting notes from captions. Where your workspace offers it and you turn it on: on a Google Meet call you start it yourself, after telling the others; a banner shows on your screen while it runs. It collects the text of Meet's own captions (everyone's words, with names), never audio, and sends the transcript to your bot only when you stop and choose to send it. Recording what others say can need their consent where you are.
  • Coding agents. If a bot runs a coding agent (such as Claude Code or Codex) on your computer and your paired browser lets that bot in, the agent can use your browser the same way the bot can, under the same rules, until the bot's task ends.
  • Notifications. To tell you when a bot needs you or finishes, the extension in Chrome registers with Google's push service (Firebase Cloud Messaging). A push carries what the notification shows, such as the step a bot wants approved or the start of its reply, plus ids to open it; Google passes it on to your browser. In Edge and Firefox there's no push service for extensions: the extension asks your Casama server once a minute instead, and nothing goes through Google.
  • Usage analytics. If you leave usage analytics on, Casama Cloud counts which features you use (for example "saved to the Library"), never page content or addresses. You can turn it off in the extension's settings or the app.

The use of information received from the extension adheres to the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. We use it only to provide the features you use, don't sell it, don't use it for advertising or creditworthiness, don't use it to train AI models, and people can't read it unless you ask us to for support, it's needed for security, or the law requires it.
